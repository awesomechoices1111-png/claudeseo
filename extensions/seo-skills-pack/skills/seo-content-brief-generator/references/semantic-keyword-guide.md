# Semantic Keyword Guide

## What Are Semantic Keywords?

Semantic keywords are terms and phrases topically related to the primary keyword. They help Google understand the full context and depth of content without requiring exact keyword matches.

Modern Google uses NLP and entity recognition. A page that covers a topic comprehensively — including related entities, concepts, and questions — signals topical authority.

---

## The Three-Tier System

### Tier 1: Must Include
Closely related synonyms, alternate phrasings, and LSI keywords. These appear in nearly every high-ranking piece on this topic.

**How to identify:**
- Google the primary keyword and scan H2/H3 headings on top 3 results
- Check Google's "Related searches" at the bottom of the SERP
- Look at bold phrases Google shows in meta descriptions on the results page

**Placement:** Throughout — H2s, H3s, body paragraphs, first 100 words, meta description

### Tier 2: Should Include
Related entities: tools, brands, people, methods, concepts. Authoritative content typically mentions these.

**How to identify:**
- What tools or brands do the top competitors mention?
- What experts, frameworks, or methodologies are cited?
- What related topics appear in "People Also Ask"?

**Placement:** Body paragraphs, examples, tool mentions, expert citations

### Tier 3: Consider Including
Long-tail variants, niche questions, emerging terms. Optional depth signals for comprehensive guides.

**How to identify:**
- AlsoAsked.com for PAA-derived long-tail questions
- AnswerThePublic for question variations
- Reddit/Quora threads for informal language patterns

**Placement:** FAQ section, advanced sections, H3 subtopics

## Semantic Keyword Discovery Methods

### Method 1: SERP Headline Mining
Open top 5 results. Copy all H2/H3 headings into a list. Cluster by theme. Recurring themes = Tier 1 semantic keywords.

### Method 2: Google Autocomplete
Type the primary keyword into Google and note all autocomplete suggestions. These are real user queries sharing semantic space.

### Method 3: Related Searches
Scroll to the bottom of the Google results page. The 8 "Related searches" are high-signal semantic keywords.

### Method 4: Entity Extraction
Read the top 3 competing articles. Highlight every proper noun, product name, person name, organization, and technical term. Include the most relevant in Tier 2.

## Placement Guide

| Article Element | Keyword Type |
|----------------|-------------|
| H1 | Primary keyword (exact or close variant) |
| H2s | Tier 1 semantic keywords, secondary keywords |
| H3s | Tier 2 entities, long-tail variants |
| Opening paragraph | Primary keyword + 1-2 Tier 1 terms |
| Body paragraphs | Natural distribution of Tier 1 and Tier 2 |
| FAQ section | Tier 3 long-tail question keywords |
| Meta description | Primary keyword + 1 Tier 1 keyword |
| Image alt text | Descriptive keyword phrases relevant to the image |
| URL slug | Primary keyword only (short form) |

---

## Avoiding Over-Optimization

Signs of keyword stuffing to warn the writer against:
- Same phrase appears in every paragraph
- Awkward phrasing to include a keyword
- URL slug is excessively long and keyword-stuffed

The goal is topical completeness, not frequency optimization. Write for the human reader — semantic coverage happens naturally when the topic is covered thoroughly.
